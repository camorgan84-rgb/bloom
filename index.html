Recovery Progress
Benefits by Body System
🧠
Brain & Mental Health
Improves: Days 4 → Month 3
▾
  • Dopamine receptor density begins increasing by day 4
  • Working memory and concentration improve by week 2
  • Anxiety and depression symptoms typically reduce by week 3–4
  • Brain reward system largely normalized by month 3
  • Long-term: creativity, problem-solving, and emotional depth return — often exceeding pre-use levels
😴
Sleep
Improves: Week 1 → Month 2
▾
  • REM sleep starts returning at week 1 (vivid dreams = healing)
  • Sleep quality measurably better by week 2–3
  • Deep slow-wave sleep stages normalize by month 1
  • Natural sleep hormone rhythms fully restored by month 2
  • Most ex-users report sleeping better long-term than they ever did while using
🫀
Heart & Circulation
Improves: Hours → Month 1
▾
  • Heart rate normalizes within hours
  • Blood pressure drops within 24 hours
  • Risk of cannabis-induced arrhythmia eliminated
  • Circulation improves, reducing cardiovascular stress
  • Exercise capacity increases as heart strain reduces over weeks
🫁
Lungs & Respiratory
Improves: Day 2 → Month 3
▾
  • Bronchial inflammation begins reducing within 48 hours
  • Cough and mucus production decrease in week 1
  • Lung capacity measurably improves by month 1
  • Respiratory infections become less frequent by month 3
  • Cannabis smoke contains many of the same carcinogens as tobacco
🧘
Mood & Anxiety
Improves: Week 1 → Week 6
▾
  • Irritability peaks at days 1–3 then improves steadily
  • Natural mood regulation returns by week 3
  • Anxiety that felt like "withdrawal" usually resolves by week 4
  • Serotonin and oxytocin rebalance, improving social motivation
  • Most people report feeling more emotionally present and stable long-term
🧬
Hormones & Metabolism
Improves: Week 2 → Month 2
▾
  • Testosterone levels increase — THC suppresses them in all genders
  • Cortisol (stress hormone) patterns normalize
  • Appetite regulation improves as ghrelin rebalances (week 1–2)
  • Blood sugar regulation improves
  • Fertility markers improve with sustained abstinence
  • Growth hormone (critical for tissue repair during sleep) increases
Supplement Protocol
🤰 Pregnancy mode is on
Most recovery supplements haven't been tested in pregnancy, and some (like ashwagandha, rhodiola and melatonin) aren't recommended. While you're pregnant, take only what your OB or midwife recommends. That's usually a prenatal vitamin with folic acid, and sometimes DHA. The full protocol comes back if you remove your due date.
Heads up: These are general wellness supplements used in recovery contexts. Always consult your doctor before starting, especially if you take medications or have health conditions.

🌊Craving Strategies8 ways to ride out an urge▾
Remember: Cravings peak in 15–20 minutes, then pass. You just have to outlast the wave.
🌊
Urge Surfing
Observe your craving like a surfer watching a wave — without riding it. Cravings are not commands. They're sensations that rise and fall.
  1. Close your eyes and notice where you feel the craving in your body
  2. Describe it — tight chest? restless hands? a pull in your gut?
  3. Breathe into it and watch it shift, change, or soften
  4. Remind yourself: "This is temporary. It will pass."
🖐️
5-4-3-2-1 Grounding
Pulls you out of the craving spiral and back into the present moment using your senses.
  1. Name 5 things you can see
  2. Name 4 things you can touch
  3. Name 3 things you can hear
  4. Name 2 things you can smell
  5. Name 1 thing you can taste
🚨
HALT Check
Many cravings are disguised basic needs. Check in before assuming you "need" to use.
  1. H — Am I Hungry?
  2. A — Am I Angry or Anxious?
  3. L — Am I Lonely?
  4. T — Am I Tired?
🏃
Move Your Body
Physical movement burns off restless energy and releases endorphins that dampen cravings. Even 5 minutes works. Want it guided? Try the Craving Flow in Recharge.
  1. Walk briskly around the block
  2. March in place or do 10 slow squats
  3. Stretch for 5 minutes
  4. Dance to one song
🌡️
Cold Water Reset
Triggers the dive reflex, rapidly slowing heart rate and clearing the mind.
  1. Splash cold water on your face
  2. Or hold ice cubes for 30 seconds
  3. Or step outside into cold air
  4. The shock interrupts the craving loop
⏱️
Delay 20 Minutes
Commit to doing nothing for just 20 minutes. Don't say never — just not right now. Most cravings collapse in this window.
  1. Set a 20-minute timer
  2. Choose a distraction: show, game, call
  3. When timer goes off, reassess
  4. Most of the time, the craving will have moved on
📞
Reach Out
Isolation fuels cravings. Connection dissolves them.
  1. Text or call someone you trust
  2. Post in a recovery community
  3. Tell them how you're feeling right now
  4. Ask them to stay on the line for a bit
✏️
Write It Out
Externalizing the craving by writing it down interrupts automatic thinking and reduces its power.
  1. Grab any paper or open notes
  2. Write: "Right now I'm feeling ____ because ____"
  3. Write what you're afraid will happen if you don't use
  4. Write one thing you're proud of since quitting

🧩CBT StrategiesTriggers, thought records, relapse plan▾
🔍
Identify Your Triggers (PPTE)
Foundation
Triggers fall into four categories. Once you know yours, you can plan around them.
  • People — Who do you use with? Who stresses you out?
  • Places — Where do you typically use? Where do you go after?
  • Things — What objects, smells, or times of day cue the urge?
  • Emotions — Boredom? Anxiety? Loneliness? Celebration?
Write your top 3 triggers below. Having them written makes the pattern visible — and visible patterns can be interrupted.
📝
Thought Record (ABC Model)
In-the-moment tool
When a craving hits, use this to slow down automatic thinking.
🧩
Challenge Cognitive Distortions
Thought patterns
Cannabis use is often maintained by predictable thinking traps. Learn to spot them:
  • "Just this once" — Is once ever really once? What does the data say?
  • "I can't handle this sober" — Have you ever handled hard things sober? You likely have.
  • "It's not like it's a real drug" — Addiction is defined by compulsion and consequences, not the substance.
  • "I need it to sleep/focus/relax" — Feels true, but these are withdrawal symptoms, not proof of need.
  • "I've already ruined it" — A slip isn't failure. It's information. Restart doesn't erase progress.

🌱
Values Clarification
Big picture
When cravings make the short-term feel overwhelming, reconnect with the long game.
  • What does freedom from cannabis give you back? (energy, money, clarity, presence)
  • What kind of person do you want to be — and does using align with that?
  • Who is watching you succeed? (even just future-you)
  • Write your "Why I'm quitting" statement and keep it somewhere you'll see it
Reading your values during a craving interrupts the dopamine chase and activates the prefrontal cortex — your wise, long-term thinking brain.
📋
Behavioral Activation
Mood & energy
Cannabis often fills time that feels empty or uncomfortable. Replace it intentionally:
  • Make a personal activity menu: 3 things you can do in 5 min, 3 in 30 min, 3 in 2+ hours
  • Schedule one enjoyable activity per day — don't wait to feel like it
  • Notice the high-risk times (evenings? weekends?) and pre-plan for them
  • Track your mood daily to see the link between activity and feeling better
🛡️
Relapse Prevention Plan
Protect your progress
Build your plan before you need it, not during a crisis.
  • Early warning signs: What thoughts/feelings signal you're at risk?
  • High-risk situations: List the top 3 and plan an exit for each
  • Emergency contact: Name one person to call when things get hard
  • If I slip: No shame spiral. Restart the clock. Ask what I can learn.
  • Reward system: What will I give myself at day 7, 30, 90, 180?
Important: A slip is not a relapse. A relapse is not a failure. Recovery is not linear. What matters is what you do next.

👆EFT TappingA guided tapping sequence for cravings▾
What is EFT?
Emotional Freedom Technique (EFT) combines gentle tapping on acupressure points with spoken affirmations. Research shows it lowers cortisol, reduces cravings, and calms the amygdala — your brain's alarm system. It can feel strange at first; that's okay. It works.
Step 1 — Setup Statement
Tap the Karate Chop point (outside edge of hand) while saying each phrase aloud 3 times:
"Even though I have this craving right now, I deeply and completely accept myself."

"Even though my body is craving relief, I choose to offer it something better."

"Even though this feels uncomfortable, I am safe, I am healing, and I am enough."
Step 2 — Tapping Sequence

Tap each point 5–7 times while saying the phrase. Repeat the full sequence 2–3 times.

1
Top of Head
Center crown of the skull
"This craving in my body..." / "This urge to escape..."
2
Eyebrow Point
Inner edge of the eyebrow, above the nose
"I'm tired of feeling this way..." / "This anxious feeling..."
3
Side of Eye
Outer corner of the eye, on the bone
"I want relief right now..." / "This restlessness..."
4
Under Eye
Under the pupil, on the cheekbone
"My body remembers the feeling..." / "This pull inside me..."
5
Under Nose
Between nose and upper lip
"Even this craving..." / "This discomfort I'm in..."
6
Chin Point
In the crease between lower lip and chin
"I'm doing the hard thing..." / "I'm choosing differently..."
7
Collarbone Point
Just below and inward from the collarbone notch
"My body is healing..." / "I'm safe without it..."
8
Under Arm
About 4 inches below the armpit
"I release what no longer serves me..." / "I'm stronger than this urge..."
9
Wrist Points
Inner wrists tapped together
"I love and accept myself..." / "Peace. Clarity. Strength."
Step 3 — Closing
Take a deep breath in and release slowly. Rate your craving intensity from 0–10. If still above 4, repeat the sequence with fresh phrases. End with:
"I am healing. I choose myself. My brain is getting clearer every day."
Move: Guided Flows
🧠
Why movement beats a craving
Read this once. It makes every flow work better.
▾
  • It feeds the same system THC used. Exercise raises anandamide, your body's own cannabinoid. That is part of the "runner's high." You are giving your endocannabinoid system a natural top-up while it rebuilds.
  • It works in real studies. In a 2011 study, daily cannabis users who were not trying to quit did ten 30-minute treadmill sessions over two weeks. Their use dropped by more than half.
  • It shrinks cravings fast. Research on short bursts of exercise (mostly in people quitting nicotine) shows craving strength falls within minutes and stays lower for a while after.
  • Long exhales flip your calm switch. Slow breathing, especially a long breath out, activates the vagus nerve and moves your body out of fight-or-flight. Every flow pairs poses with breath for this reason.
  • It trains the urge-surfing muscle. Holding a pose means feeling a sensation without reacting to it. That is exactly the skill you use to ride out a craving.
Go gently. Skip anything that hurts, and check with your doctor if you have an injury, are pregnant, or have a heart condition.

Quick Practices
One guided exercise, 1 to 2 minutes. Use them on ordinary days so they work better on hard ones.
Mindfulness Exercises
🧍
Body Scan Meditation
Builds the skill of noticing sensation without needing to fix or escape it — the core muscle of riding out a craving.
  1. Lie down or sit comfortably, eyes closed
  2. Bring attention to your toes — just notice, don't change anything
  3. Slowly move attention up through feet, legs, torso, arms, head (20–30 sec each)
  4. If your mind wanders, gently bring it back to the body
🍇
Mindful Eating Reset
Retrains attention and slows down the same "reach for something to change how I feel" impulse that drives use.
  1. Pick one small food item
  2. Look at it, smell it, notice its texture before eating
  3. Take one bite, chew slowly, set it down between bites
  4. Notice flavor changing as you chew
💛
Self-Compassion Break
For the moments guilt or shame about a craving (or a slip) show up. Shame fuels relapse; compassion interrupts it.
  1. Name it: "This is a hard moment"
  2. Normalize it: "Struggling is part of recovery, not a sign I'm failing"
  3. Hand on heart (or chest), say: "May I be kind to myself right now"
🚶
Mindful Walking
Combines movement (which burns off craving energy) with grounding attention.
  1. Walk slightly slower than normal
  2. Feel each foot contact the ground
  3. Match your breath to your steps
  4. Name 3 things you notice along the way
Nervous System Regulation
Your vagus nerve is the main line between brain and body for calming down. These tools directly stimulate it — they're not just "relaxing," they physiologically shift you out of fight-or-flight.
🐝
Humming / Bee Breath
The vibration from humming directly stimulates the vagus nerve where it runs through the throat.
  1. Close your mouth, breathe in through the nose
  2. Hum steadily on the exhale until your breath runs out
  3. Feel the vibration in your throat and chest
  4. Repeat 5–10 rounds
🦋
Butterfly Hug
Alternating bilateral tapping is used in trauma therapy to calm the nervous system and can be done anywhere, discreetly.
  1. Cross your arms over your chest, hands on opposite shoulders
  2. Slowly alternate tapping left, then right
  3. Breathe slowly as you tap for 1–2 minutes
👀
Orienting / Safety Scan
A polyvagal technique that tells your nervous system "no threat here" by re-engaging your visual sense of the present room.
  1. Slowly turn your head left, then right
  2. Let your eyes land on a few objects — really see them
  3. Silently note: "I am here. I am safe right now."
🎶
Vocal Toning
Singing, gargling, and chanting all use the same throat muscles that stimulate the vagus nerve.
  1. Sing along loudly to one full song
  2. Or gargle water for 30 seconds
  3. Or chant a long "om" for a few breaths
Sleep Toolkit
Heads up: Disrupted sleep and vivid dreams are common in the first 1–4 weeks as your natural REM cycles return (see Recovery Progress in Health). It's temporary, and it's a sign of healing, not a problem.
🌙
Wind-Down Routine
Doing the same sequence nightly trains your brain to associate it with sleep onset.
  1. Pick a 30–60 min pre-bed window
  2. Dim the lights in the house
  3. Warm shower or a sleep-support tea (see Recipes below)
  4. Light stretching or a few lines in your journal
📵
Digital Sunset
Screen blue light suppresses melatonin right when you need it rising.
  1. Stop phone/TV use 60 minutes before bed
  2. Switch devices to night mode earlier in the evening
  3. Swap scrolling for a physical book or audio
🛏️
The 20-Minute Rule
Lying awake and frustrated teaches your brain that bed = anxiety. Break that link.
  1. If you're not asleep in ~20 minutes, get up
  2. Do something calm and low-light (not your phone)
  3. Return to bed only when you feel sleepy
Stress Toolkit
📝
Brain Dump
Gets swirling thoughts out of your head and onto paper where they're easier to manage.
  1. Set a 5-minute timer
  2. Write every worry or task, no editing, no order
  3. Close the notebook when the timer ends
⏳
Scheduled Worry Time
Instead of trying to stop worrying (which backfires), give it a designated window.
  1. Pick a daily 15-minute "worry time"
  2. When a worry pops up outside that window, jot it down for later
  3. Actually think it through during the scheduled time — no earlier
🌳
Nature Dose
Time outdoors measurably lowers cortisol — no special technique required.
  1. Get outside for 10–20 minutes
  2. Leave your phone in your pocket
  3. Notice something natural: sky, trees, wind, birds
🙅
Boundary Check-In
Overcommitting is a quiet, constant stress source that often goes unnoticed.
  1. Ask: "Is this actually mine to carry right now?"
  2. Practice saying no to one nonessential ask this week
  3. Notice how it feels afterward, not just before
🎵
Music Regulation
Slow-tempo music can gently pull your heart rate down to match it.
  1. Build a "calm down" playlist ahead of time
  2. Favor slower tracks (roughly 60–80 bpm)
  3. Put it on at the first sign of tension, don't wait for a crisis
🗣️
Name It to Tame It
Putting a specific word on an emotion measurably lowers the brain's alarm response.
  1. Pause and ask: "What exactly am I feeling?"
  2. Get specific — not "bad," but "overwhelmed" or "on edge"
  3. Say or write it in one sentence: "I feel ___ because ___"
Motivation Toolkit
🪞
Future Self Letter
Makes your "why" concrete instead of abstract.
  1. Picture yourself one year clean
  2. Write that version of you a letter about what changed
  3. Keep it somewhere you'll reread it on a hard day
🧱
Habit Stacking
New habits stick far better when attached to something you already do daily.
  1. Pick one existing daily habit (brushing teeth, making coffee)
  2. Attach one recovery habit right after it (one gratitude line, a breathing round)
  3. Keep the stack the same every day until it's automatic
🏅
Reward Ladder
Plan rewards in advance so motivation doesn't depend on willpower alone.
  1. Day 7: something small and immediate (a favorite meal, a movie night)
  2. Day 30: something you've been putting off (new gear, a class)
  3. Day 90: something bigger (a trip, a bigger purchase)
  4. Day 180+: something that marks the milestone permanently
🧍
Identity Statements
Identity-based language holds up better under pressure than willpower-based language.
  1. Notice when you think "I'm trying to quit"
  2. Swap it for "I'm someone who doesn't use"
  3. Say it out loud once a day, especially on hard days
📣
Accountability Ping
External accountability is one of the strongest predictors of sticking with recovery.
  1. Pick one person you trust
  2. Send them your day count today, even if it's small
  3. Ask them to check in on a specific hard day (like a weekend)
🎯
Visualize the Win
Mental rehearsal of a specific future moment makes the goal feel real, not abstract.
  1. Close your eyes for 60 seconds
  2. Picture one specific future moment this makes possible
  3. Notice details: where you are, who's there, how you feel

Sheet-Pan Salmon & Sweet Potato
1 salmon fillet, 1 sweet potato in cubes, 1 cup broccoli, 1 tbsp olive oil, salt, pepper, lemon. Roast potato at 400°F for 10 min, add salmon and broccoli, roast 12 to 15 min more. Serves 1, about 30 min.
Salmon brings omega-3 fats for brain cell health plus protein with tryptophan. Sweet potato is a slow carb that helps your brain use that tryptophan to make serotonin. One pan, little cleanup.

Turkey & Black Bean Chili
1 lb ground turkey, 1 onion, 2 cloves garlic, 1 can black beans, 1 can diced tomatoes, 1 tbsp chili powder, 1 tsp cumin. Brown turkey with onion and garlic, add the rest, simmer 25 min. Serves 4.
Turkey and beans pair protein with lots of fiber, the combo that flattens blood sugar swings. A big batch means dinner is already decided on the nights you are too tired to cook, which are often the riskiest nights.

Chickpea & Spinach Curry
1 can chickpeas, 1 can coconut milk, 2 big handfuls spinach, 1 onion, 2 tbsp curry powder, 1 tsp grated ginger. Soften onion, stir in spices, add chickpeas and coconut milk, simmer 15 min, wilt in spinach. Serve over rice. Serves 3.
Chickpeas and spinach are rich in magnesium and folate, both linked to steadier mood and better sleep. Fully plant-based and cheap to make.
🍳
15-Minute Veggie Egg Fried Rice
1 cup cooked rice (day-old works best), 2 eggs, 1 cup frozen mixed veg, 1 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp sesame oil, green onion. Scramble eggs, set aside, fry veg and rice, toss everything with soy and sesame oil.
Eggs carry choline, which your brain uses to make acetylcholine, a messenger involved in memory and focus. Fast enough to beat the urge to order takeout or reach for the old routine.

If snacks used to go with getting high, the snack is part of the habit. Picking a new go-to keeps the comfort and drops the routine it was tied to.
🍿
Crunchy: Olive Oil Popcorn
3 cups air-popped popcorn, 1 tsp olive oil, pinch of salt, optional nutritional yeast or smoked paprika
Popcorn is a whole grain with real fiber, and a big bowl is low in calories. It scratches the crunch itch that chips usually fill.
🍇
Sweet: Frozen Grapes + Dark Chocolate
A handful of frozen grapes, or 1 to 2 squares of 70%+ dark chocolate with a few almonds
Frozen grapes eat like candy but slowly. Dark chocolate has magnesium, and the almonds add protein and fat so the sugar hits softer.
🫘
Salty: Roasted Chickpeas
1 can chickpeas (rinsed and patted dry), 1 tbsp olive oil, salt, garlic powder. Roast at 400°F for 25 to 30 min, shaking halfway.
Crunchy and salty like pretzels, but with protein and fiber that actually fill you up instead of leaving you wanting more.
🥄
Creamy: Greek Yogurt Bowl
¾ cup Greek yogurt, 1 tsp honey, cinnamon, a spoon of nut butter or granola on top
Thick, cold and creamy like ice cream, with about three times the protein. Protein is the most filling nutrient and the best at keeping blood sugar steady.
